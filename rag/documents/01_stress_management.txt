TITLE: Stress Management for College Students
CATEGORY: Stress
PURPOSE: RAG knowledge tied to the weekly PSS-10 monitoring questions

1. OVERVIEW
Section 1 of weekly monitoring is the Perceived Stress Scale (PSS-10). Students rate how often they felt or thought a certain way. The form uses Never, Almost Never, Sometimes, Fairly Often, Very Often.

The system treats the PSS total as one MFBI factor, not as a diagnosis of anxiety, depression, or burnout. Higher scores mean the student reported more perceived stress. Items 4, 5, 7, and 8 are reverse-scored: feeling confident, that things were going their way, able to control irritations, or on top of things lowers the stress score.

Score bands used by the system: 0–13 Low · 14–26 Moderate · 27–40 High.

Recommendations should answer the actual PSS themes the student just rated, not generic “manage stress” slogans.

2. MONITORING QUESTIONS AND GUIDANCE
Q1. In the last month, how often have you been upset because of something that happened unexpectedly?
When this is elevated, help the student name one unexpected school event this week (a surprise quiz, a changed deadline, a group delay) and write the next small response. A short pause before reacting is enough. Do not tell them they should never feel upset.

Q2. In the last month, how often have you felt that you were unable to control the important things in your life?
When this is elevated, shrink the week to what they can control: one deadline, one class, one message to a teacher. Put the rest on a list so it is not all “in their head.”

Q3. In the last month, how often have you felt nervous and stressed?
When this is elevated, offer a body-and-task reset: two slow breaths, a real meal or short walk, then one under-one-hour next step. Do not diagnose a panic disorder.

Q4. In the last month, how often have you felt confident about your ability to handle your personal problems? (reverse)
A low rating here (which raises stress) means confidence is thin. Suggest finishing one small required piece so they can see proof of handling something. Do not say “just be more confident.”

Q5. In the last month, how often have you felt that things were going your way? (reverse)
A low rating means the week feels stacked against them. Help them pick the soonest due item so one thing can go their way today.

Q6. In the last month, how often have you found that you could not cope with all the things that you had to do?
When this is elevated, they do not need more hours. They need a shorter list: keep the required work, delay optional extras, and break “all the things” into named steps.

Q7. In the last month, how often have you been able to control irritations in your life? (reverse)
A low rating means small frictions are adding up. Suggest a stop-time for schoolwork and one break between classes so irritations are not carried into the next task.

Q8. In the last month, how often have you felt that you were on top of things? (reverse)
A low rating means they feel behind. A one-page list of this week’s due dates, then one file for the next hour, is the practical “on top of things” move.

Q9. In the last month, how often have you been angered because of things that happened that were outside of your control?
When this is elevated, acknowledge the feeling without diagnosing. Point them to what they can still do (clarify a requirement, send one group message, ask a teacher) and what they can park until later.

Q10. In the last month, how often have you felt difficulties were piling up so high that you could not overcome them?
When this is elevated, the pile is the target. Write every due item down, circle the top one, and do a piece that can be finished in under an hour. If the pile still feels impossible, talking with a teacher, adviser, or the Guidance Office is appropriate.

3. PERSONALIZATION RULES
If STRESS is high:
- Prioritize tips that match Q3, Q6, and Q10 (nervous/stressed, cannot cope with all the things, difficulties piling up).
- Give two practical stress tips. Check sleep and workload next.
- Do not auto-refer to counseling unless High or Severe overall risk, or the student asks.

If STRESS is moderate:
- One or two tips from Q1–Q3 or Q8 (unexpected events, control, nervousness, not on top of things).
- Encourage completing next week’s monitoring.

If STRESS is low:
- One sentence only. Do not imply they have no school difficulty.

4. SAFETY AND LIMITATIONS
Do not diagnose. Do not replace a counselor. If the student describes a safety concern, direct them to verified human help. Do not invent contacts.

6. STUDENT-FACING GUIDANCE
Write like a caring adviser sitting with the student. Use "you." Do not diagnose. Do not start every tip with "If you."

HOW THIS MAY FEEL
- Unexpected surprises — a quiz, a changed deadline, a group delay — can leave you rattled even when you are trying.
- Important things may feel out of your hands, as if the week is running you instead of the other way around.
- Nervousness can sit in your body: tight chest, restless thoughts, a sense that you cannot catch up.
- When confidence is thin, even ordinary tasks look bigger than they are.
- Difficulties can feel piled so high that starting anywhere seems pointless.

THINGS YOU CAN TRY THIS WEEK
- Name the one surprise or deadline that is sitting heaviest, then write the next small step for it.
- Keep only this week's required work on one list so the rest is not looping in your head.
- Pause for two slow breaths, eat or take a short walk, then give one task an honest hour.
- Finish one small required piece today so you have proof that something actually moved.
- Pick the item due soonest so one thing can go your way before you add more.
- Drop optional extras and break "everything" into named steps you can finish in under an hour.
- Set a stop time for schoolwork and take a real break between classes so irritations do not pile into the next task.
- Write due dates on one page and open only one file for the next hour — that is enough to feel a little more on top of things.
- If the pile still feels impossible, tell a teacher you trust, your adviser, or someone in Guidance. You do not have to sort it alone.
