TITLE: Advice for short or restless sleep
CATEGORY: Sleep
PURPOSE: Advice tied to the Sleep Hours questionnaire items in weekly monitoring

1. YOUR FORM
Variable: Sleep
Questionnaire: Sleep Hours
The form asks how you slept this week. Higher sleep risk means poorer rest. This is wellness advice, not a medical diagnosis of insomnia.
Common answer styles on the form:
- Hours slept: less than 5 hours, 5–6, 6–7, 7–8, more than 8
- Consistency or rest: from very inconsistent / not rested to very consistent / fully rested
- How often lack of sleep hurt school: Never to Very often
- Some forms use Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree for sleep statements
Most adults need at least 7 hours of sleep.

2. Q1. On average, how many hours did you sleep per night this week?
Choices on many forms: 1 = less than 5 hours, 2 = 5–6 hours, 3 = 6–7 hours, 4 = 7–8 hours, 5 = more than 8 hours.
How this may feel: Short nights can leave you coming to class already tired, even when you are trying.
Advice for next week: Try to sleep about 7 hours and keep a bedtime you can keep, even if one homework is not done. Do not recommend an all-nighter. Tired studying usually costs more than it gives back.

3. Q2. How consistent was your sleep schedule this week?
Choices on many forms: 1 = Very inconsistent … 5 = Very consistent.
How this may feel: Sleep and wake times that keep changing make mornings feel rushed and evenings feel too long.
Advice for next week: Keep sleep and wake time closer to the same each day, even after a short night. Sleeping very late can make the next night worse. Move one study block earlier so bedtime can stay closer to normal.

4. Q3. How rested did you feel upon waking?
Choices on many forms: 1 = Not rested … 5 = Fully rested.
How this may feel: Waking still tired often means the day starts before your body is ready.
Advice for next week: Skip late coffee or energy drinks, dim the phone, and keep your bed for sleep, not homework. Set a stop time for schoolwork. A short wind-down beats another chapter after midnight.

5. Q4. How often did lack of sleep affect your academics this week?
Choices on many forms: 1 = Never … 5 = Very often. Agreeing that lack of sleep hurt school raises sleep risk.
How this may feel: When lack of sleep hurts classwork, studying later usually makes the next day worse.
Advice for next week: Treat rest as part of studying. Shorten the latest study session. Use a focused block earlier in the day. If sleep keeps harming school after you try this, ask a teacher, adviser, or a real health person at school. Do not invent medicines.

6. OTHER SLEEP WORDING ON THE FORM
If the form says you had difficulty sleeping, trouble sleeping, or could not sleep: keep a bedtime you can keep, get up briefly if you cannot fall asleep, and do not study in bed.
If the form says you slept enough, felt rested, or kept a regular schedule: keep the bedtime that is working, including on busy school nights.

7. ADVICE WHEN SLEEP RISK IS HIGH
Mention Q1 and Q2 first (hours and schedule), then Q3 and Q4 (not rested, academics affected).
Give more than one sleep tip. Do not only say "sleep more."
Never tell them to give up sleep to finish workload or add study hours.

8. ADVICE WHEN SLEEP RISK IS MODERATE
One or two tips from Q1 or Q2: hours or a steadier bedtime.

9. ADVICE WHEN SLEEP LOOKS OKAY
One kind sentence only. Focus advice on the harder parts of next week.

10. STUDENT-FACING GUIDANCE
Write like a caring adviser. Use "you." Use simple, basic words. Short sentences. Do not diagnose.
Match advice to the Sleep Hours questions the student answered. Do not start every tip with "If you."

HOW THIS MAY FEEL
- Q1: Short nights (under about 7 hours) can leave you coming to class already tired.
- Q2: An inconsistent sleep schedule makes mornings feel rushed and evenings feel too long.
- Q3: Not feeling rested upon waking means the day starts before your body is ready.
- Q4: When lack of sleep affects your academics, studying later usually makes the next day worse.

THINGS YOU CAN TRY NEXT WEEK
- Q1: Try to sleep about 7 hours and keep a bedtime you can keep, even if one homework is not done.
- Q2: Keep sleep and wake time closer to the same each day, even after a short night. Sleeping very late can make the next night worse.
- Q3: Skip late coffee or energy drinks, dim the phone, and keep your bed for sleep, not homework.
- Q4: Move one study block earlier and stop working in time to slow down. Rest is part of doing well in class, not a break from school.
- Write tomorrow's first small task on paper, then close the laptop so your mind can stop going round and round.
