TITLE: Advice for a heavy classwork week
CATEGORY: Academic Workload
PURPOSE: Advice tied to the five Academic Workload questionnaire items in weekly monitoring

1. YOUR FORM
Variable: Academic Workload
Questionnaire: Academic Workload
Answer choices: Definitely Disagree, Disagree, Neutral, Agree, Definitely Agree
The form asks how this week's schoolwork felt. Higher agreement means a heavier load. This is not a count of units and not a diagnosis.

2. Q1. How heavy is your current academic workload this week?
How this may feel: Next week's schoolwork can feel heavier than the hours you actually have.
Advice for next week: Put assignments, quizzes, projects, and readings on one list. Rank by due date. Leave gaps for sleep and meals. Do not say "work harder."

3. Q2. How often do overlapping deadlines make your workload hard to manage?
How this may feel: When due dates pile up on the same days, it can feel like five unfinished files are open at once.
Advice for next week: Finish what is due soonest, then a small piece of the larger project. One page of due dates stops you jumping between five files. Ask a teacher early if two big items truly clash.

4. Q3. How often do assignments require more time than you expected?
How this may feel: Homework that takes longer than you thought can make you feel behind even when you are working.
Advice for next week: Split the assignment into outline, draft, check, and submit. Use the next hour for only one of those steps. Do not make an all-nighter the default fix.

5. Q4. How often do you feel pressured by the volume of academic requirements?
How this may feel: There can be so many requirements that the list never looks smaller.
Advice for next week: Keep required items. Delay extra work. Close extra tabs. One main task per hour. Pressure eases when the list is visible and shorter.

6. Q5. How difficult is it to keep up with quizzes, projects, and readings this week?
How this may feel: Quizzes, projects, and readings can blend into one pile that never gets smaller.
Advice for next week: Give each type its own short block. Quiz: a few questions. Project: one small milestone. Reading: one chapter or one lecture. If you still cannot keep up, asking the teacher or adviser is a planning step.

7. ADVICE WHEN WORKLOAD IS HIGH
Lead with Q1, Q2, and Q5: heavy week, overlapping deadlines, and keeping up with quizzes, projects, and readings.
Check sleep. Do not tell them to study more hours.

8. ADVICE WHEN WORKLOAD IS MODERATE
One list from Q1, then one next piece of the soonest due item from Q2.

9. ADVICE WHEN WORKLOAD LOOKS OKAY
One kind sentence only. Focus advice on the harder parts of next week.

10. STUDENT-FACING GUIDANCE
Write like a caring adviser. Use "you." Use simple, basic words. Short sentences. Do not diagnose.
Match advice to the Academic Workload questions the student answered. Do not start every tip with "If you."

HOW THIS MAY FEEL
- Q1: Your current academic workload this week can feel heavier than the hours you actually have.
- Q2: Overlapping deadlines can make the load hard to manage, like five unfinished files at once.
- Q3: Assignments that require more time than you expected can make you feel behind even when you are working.
- Q4: The volume of academic requirements can feel pressuring.
- Q5: It can be hard to keep up with quizzes, projects, and readings this week.

THINGS YOU CAN TRY NEXT WEEK
- Q1: Put every quiz, project, and reading on one page with due dates, then circle what is due first. Leave space for sleep and a meal.
- Q2: Do the soonest item, then a small piece of the bigger project — not five files at the same time. Tell a teacher early if two big items fall on the same day.
- Q3: Split a long assignment into outline, draft, check, and submit, and use the next hour for only one of those steps.
- Q4: Keep only what is required next week. Close extra tabs so the list looks smaller.
- Q5: Give quizzes, projects, and readings their own short blocks instead of mixing them all night.
