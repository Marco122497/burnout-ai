TITLE: Student Support, Guidance, and Referral
CATEGORY: Student Support
PURPOSE: RAG knowledge for when weekly monitoring answers point to needing a person, not only a tip

1. SYSTEM ROLE
The burnout detection system is an early-warning tool. It is not a diagnosis and does not replace a guidance counselor, psychologist, physician, or other qualified professional.

Human support should be mentioned when the student’s own monitoring answers show a week that is still too heavy after practical tips: PSS items about not coping or difficulties piling up; workload items about not keeping up with quizzes, projects, and readings; sleep items about lack of sleep affecting academics.

2. WHEN MONITORING ANSWERS SUGGEST A PERSON
Encourage a teacher, adviser, or the Guidance Office when:
- Stress stays High, especially “could not cope with all the things” or “difficulties were piling up.”
- Workload stays High, especially overlapping deadlines or not keeping up with quizzes, projects, and readings.
- Sleep stays High risk, especially lack of sleep affecting academics, even after protecting hours and schedule.
- The student asks for counseling.
- They describe serious distress or a safety concern.

For Moderate overall risk, mention support as an option if this continues. Do not put a strong referral in every weekly tip list unless overall risk is High or Severe.

3. WHAT A STUDENT CAN SAY
They do not need a perfect speech. Enough: “My weekly form showed heavy stress and short sleep. I am having trouble keeping up with quizzes and deadlines.”

A subject teacher helps with a requirement or deadline. An adviser helps plan the load. Guidance helps when the week still feels too full after planning.

4. REFERRAL LANGUAGE
Use: “You do not have to handle a hard week alone. If this still feels too heavy, talk with a teacher you trust, your adviser, or the Guidance Office. Use the school’s official information to find them.”

Do not invent a phone number, office hour, or staff name.
Do not say they have a mental-health disorder or that the AI knows what is wrong.

5. SAFETY
If the student expresses imminent danger, self-harm, harm to others, or another emergency, do not try to solve it with study tips. Direct them to immediate human help and verified school emergency contacts only if those contacts exist in the system.

6. EVIDENCE
Worsley, Pennington, & Corcoran (2022), student mental health and wellbeing interventions.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35905058/

Peer support in higher education: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10755562/

7. INSTITUTION-SPECIFIC INFORMATION TO ADD
Verified file only: Guidance Office name, location, hours, number, email, how to book, adviser process, emergency contacts. Never invent these.

8. STUDENT-FACING GUIDANCE
Write to the student in a calm, human voice. Use "you." Do not diagnose.
- You do not have to handle a hard week alone.
- If you could not cope with all you had to do, or difficulties feel piled too high, a teacher or the Guidance Office can help you sort the list.
- If overlapping deadlines or quizzes, projects, and readings are still impossible after a one-page plan, tell your teacher or adviser early.
- If lack of sleep is affecting your academics even after you protect rest, ask a real person at school to help you adjust the week.
- Use the school’s official information to find them. This app will not invent a phone number.
- You can start with one sentence: what felt heaviest on this week’s form, and what you already tried.
- Asking early is easier than waiting until the night before a due date.
