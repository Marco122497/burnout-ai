TITLE: Advice for a worried, tense week
CATEGORY: Stress
PURPOSE: Advice tied to the Perceived Stress Scale (PSS-10) questionnaire in weekly monitoring

1. YOUR FORM
Variable: Stress
Questionnaire: Perceived Stress Scale (PSS-10)
Intro on the form: The PSS asks about your feelings and thoughts during the last month. In each case, indicate how often you felt or thought a certain way.
Answer choices: Never, Almost Never, Sometimes, Fairly Often, Very Often
Score: 0–40 after reverse scoring. 0–13 Low · 14–26 Moderate · 27–40 High
Reverse-scored items: Q4, Q5, Q7, Q8. Low confidence, things not going your way, not controlling irritations, or not on top of things raise stress.
Stress is how the week feels in your mind and body. It is not Academic Workload, Study Time, or Sleep Hours. Do not turn stress advice into only a homework list.

2. Q1. In the last month, how often have you been upset because of something that happened unexpectedly?
How this may feel: A surprise quiz, a changed due date, or a group delay can leave you upset even when you are trying.
Advice for next week: Name what happened and how it felt. Pause before you react. Then choose one next step. Feeling upset is okay.

3. Q2. In the last month, how often have you felt that you were unable to control the important things in your life?
How this may feel: Important things may feel out of your hands, as if next week is running you.
Advice for next week: Shrink next week to what you can still choose. One deadline, one class, or one message. Write the rest down so worry is not doing the planning.

4. Q3. In the last month, how often have you felt nervous and stressed?
How this may feel: You may feel tense: tight chest, restless thoughts, a sense that you cannot catch up.
Advice for next week: Take two slow breaths, eat, or take a short walk. Then give one task one honest hour. This is not a diagnosis.

5. Q4. In the last month, how often have you felt confident about your ability to handle your personal problems?
This item is reverse-scored. A low rating raises your stress score.
How this may feel: When you do not feel sure of yourself, even small tasks look bigger than they are.
Advice for next week: Finish one small required piece so you have proof you can handle something. Do not say "just be more confident."

6. Q5. In the last month, how often have you felt that things were going your way?
This item is reverse-scored. A low rating raises your stress score.
How this may feel: The week can feel stacked against you.
Advice for next week: Pick the item due first so one thing can go your way before you add more.

7. Q6. In the last month, how often have you found that you could not cope with all the things that you had to do?
How this may feel: "All the things" can feel like too much to carry.
Advice for next week: You do not need more hours. Keep required work. Delay extra work. Break the list into small steps you can finish in under an hour.

8. Q7. In the last month, how often have you been able to control irritations in your life?
This item is reverse-scored. A low rating raises your stress score.
How this may feel: Small annoyances spill into the next class or the next file.
Advice for next week: Set a stop time for schoolwork. Take a real break between classes so one irritation is not carried into the next hour.

9. Q8. In the last month, how often have you felt that you were on top of things?
This item is reverse-scored. A low rating raises your stress score.
How this may feel: You may feel behind, like the week is already ahead of you.
Advice for next week: Write next week's due dates on one page. Open only one file for the next hour. That is enough to feel a little more on top of things.

10. Q9. In the last month, how often have you been angered because of things that happened that were outside of your control?
How this may feel: Anger at things you cannot change is allowed.
Advice for next week: Do one thing you can still do, such as ask a teacher or send one group message. Park the rest. Do not argue the feeling away.

11. Q10. In the last month, how often have you felt difficulties were piling up so high that you could not overcome them?
How this may feel: Problems can feel piled so high that starting anywhere seems pointless.
Advice for next week: Write every due item down, circle the top one, and finish a piece in under an hour. If the pile still feels too big, tell a teacher you trust, your adviser, or someone in Guidance.

12. ADVICE WHEN STRESS IS HIGH
Give two PSS tips first: one for Q3 (nervous and stressed) that settles the body, and one for Q6 or Q10 (cannot cope / difficulties piling up) that is a small next step.
Do not replace this with only a to-do list.

13. ADVICE WHEN STRESS IS MODERATE
One or two tips from Q1, Q2, Q3, or Q8.

14. ADVICE WHEN STRESS LOOKS OKAY
One kind sentence only. Do not imply they have no school difficulty.

15. STUDENT-FACING GUIDANCE
Write like a caring adviser. Use "you." Use simple, basic words. Short sentences. Do not diagnose.
Match advice to the PSS-10 questions the student answered. Do not start every tip with "If you."

- Q6: You may feel you cannot cope with all the things you had to do.

- Q8: You may not feel on top of things.
- Q9: You may feel angry about things outside your control.
- Q10: Difficulties may feel piled so high that starting anywhere seems pointless.
