TITLE: Study Time and Effective Learning
CATEGORY: Study Time
PURPOSE: RAG knowledge for the Academic Burnout Detection and Early Warning System

1. OVERVIEW
Study time represents the amount of time a student spends studying or preparing for classes outside scheduled class hours. The system should not assume that more study hours automatically indicate better learning.

Excessively long study periods may also reduce time available for sleep and recovery. The recommendation engine should therefore consider study time together with workload, sleep, and stress.

2. EFFECTIVE STUDY STRATEGIES
A. Retrieval practice.
Students can test themselves on information rather than relying only on rereading. Retrieval practice has evidence supporting improved later recall.

Examples:
- Practice questions
- Flashcards
- Explaining a concept without looking at notes
- Short self-tests

B. Distributed practice.
When practical, learning can be distributed across multiple sessions instead of placing all learning into one very long session.

C. Define a session goal.
Before studying, identify what should be completed or understood.

D. Break large material into manageable units.
This makes it easier to monitor progress.

E. Use appropriate breaks.
Breaks can help prevent excessively prolonged study sessions and support recovery.

3. STUDY-TIME PERSONALIZATION
If STUDY TIME is high and SLEEP is low:
- Recommend reviewing the schedule and protecting sleep.
- Focus on study efficiency rather than adding more hours.

If STUDY TIME is high and STRESS is high:
- Retrieve stress-management and study-efficiency guidance.
- Encourage prioritization and active learning.

If STUDY TIME is high and WORKLOAD is high:
- Retrieve workload-management guidance.
- Recommend prioritizing tasks and using effective study methods.

If STUDY TIME is normal:
- Do not assume additional study time is required.
- Focus on other elevated factors.

4. WHAT THE SYSTEM SHOULD NOT SAY
Avoid:
- "You need to study X hours every day."
- "More study hours always produce better grades."
- "You should study all night."
- "Sleep less so you can study more."

Study requirements vary by course, student, assessment, and institution.

6. STUDENT-FACING GUIDANCE
Write to the student in a calm, human voice. Use "you." Do not diagnose.
- Study in shorter focused blocks with a clear goal, then take a real break.
- Test yourself with a few questions or by explaining an idea out loud instead of rereading for hours.
- If long study nights are stealing sleep, shorten one session this week rather than adding more hours.
- Progress can be a small finished piece, not an all-nighter.
