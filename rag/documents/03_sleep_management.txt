TITLE: Advice for short or restless sleep
CATEGORY: Sleep
PURPOSE: Advice tied to the Sleep Hours questionnaire items in weekly monitoring

1. YOUR FORM
Variable: Sleep
Questionnaire: Sleep Hours
The form asks how you slept this week. Higher sleep risk means poorer rest. This is wellness advice, not a medical diagnosis of insomnia.
Answer style: Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree.
Questions 1 and 2 are positive. Disagreeing means sleep was not enough or did not feel good, so sleep risk goes up.
Questions 3, 4, and 5 are negative. Agreeing means sleep was harder, so those items are reverse scored and sleep risk goes up.
Most adults need enough sleep to function the next day, often about 7 hours.

2. Q1. During the past week, I obtained enough actual sleep each night.
Choices: 1 = Strongly Disagree … 5 = Strongly Agree. Disagreeing raises sleep risk.
How this may feel: Nights that were not long enough can leave you coming to class already tired, even when you are trying.
Advice for next week: Keep a bedtime that gives you enough sleep, even if one homework is not done. Do not recommend an all-nighter. Tired studying usually costs more than it gives back.

3. Q2. During the past week, I considered my overall sleep quality to be good.
Choices: 1 = Strongly Disagree … 5 = Strongly Agree. Disagreeing raises sleep risk.
How this may feel: Sleep that did not feel good can make the next day start before your body is ready.
Advice for next week: Skip late coffee or energy drinks, dim the phone, and keep your bed for sleep, not homework. A short wind-down beats another chapter after midnight.

4. Q3. During the past week, I had difficulty falling asleep at night.
Choices: 1 = Strongly Disagree … 5 = Strongly Agree. Agreeing raises sleep risk.
How this may feel: Lying awake makes the night feel long, and the morning comes too soon.
Advice for next week: Stop schoolwork before bed. If you cannot fall asleep, get up briefly, then go back when you feel sleepy. Do not study in bed.

5. Q4. During the past week, I had difficulty staying awake while doing activities during the day.
Choices: 1 = Strongly Disagree … 5 = Strongly Agree. Agreeing raises sleep risk.
How this may feel: Fighting sleep in class or while studying means the night did not give you enough rest.
Advice for next week: Move one study block earlier. Do not add a late session to catch up. A short daytime rest is not a substitute for enough sleep at night.

6. Q5. During the past week, I had difficulty getting enough sleep to function well during the day.
Choices: 1 = Strongly Disagree … 5 = Strongly Agree. Agreeing raises sleep risk.
How this may feel: When sleep is not enough to function, studying later usually makes the next day worse.
Advice for next week: Treat rest as part of studying. Shorten the latest study session. If you still cannot function well after you protect sleep, ask a teacher, adviser, or a real health person at school. Do not invent medicines.

7. ADVICE WHEN SLEEP RISK IS HIGH
Mention not enough sleep and poor sleep quality first, then trouble falling asleep, staying awake in the day, and not functioning well.
Give more than one sleep tip. Do not only say "sleep more."
Never tell them to give up sleep to finish workload or add study hours.

8. ADVICE WHEN SLEEP RISK IS MODERATE
One or two tips: enough sleep at night, or an easier time falling asleep.

9. ADVICE WHEN SLEEP LOOKS OKAY
One kind sentence only. Focus advice on the harder parts of next week. Sleep does not come first when its score is Low.

9A. WHEN TO PRIORITIZE SLEEP
If sleep risk is the highest of the four scores, name it first. Example: sleep 0.90 high comes before academic workload 0.75 high, stress 0.68 high, and study time 0.18 low. Say the student should prioritize sleep, then the other High factors. Do not tell them to give up sleep to finish schoolwork.

10. STUDENT-FACING GUIDANCE
Write like a caring adviser. Use "you." Use simple, basic words. Short sentences. Do not diagnose.
Match advice to the Sleep Hours questions the student answered. Do not start every tip with "If you."

HOW THIS MAY FEEL
- Not getting enough actual sleep can leave you coming to class already tired.
- Sleep that did not feel good means the day starts before your body is ready.
- Difficulty falling asleep makes the night feel long.
- Difficulty staying awake during the day means the night did not give you enough rest.
- Not enough sleep to function well means studying later usually makes the next day worse.

THINGS YOU CAN TRY NEXT WEEK
- Keep a bedtime that gives you enough sleep, even if one homework is not done.
- Skip late coffee or energy drinks, dim the phone, and keep your bed for sleep, not homework.
- Stop schoolwork before bed. If you cannot fall asleep, get up briefly, then go back when you feel sleepy.
- If you are sleepy during class or activities, move one study block earlier instead of working late.
- Shorten the latest study session so you can function the next day. Rest is part of doing well in class, not a break from school.

11. RETRIEVAL KEYWORDS
Sleep Hours, enough actual sleep each night, overall sleep quality, difficulty falling asleep, difficulty staying awake, activities during the day, enough sleep to function well, Strongly Disagree, Strongly Agree, bedtime, advice
