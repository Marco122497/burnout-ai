TITLE: Advice for long study hours
CATEGORY: Study Time
PURPOSE: Advice tied to the Study Time questionnaire items in weekly monitoring

1. YOUR FORM
Variable: Study Time
Questionnaire: Study Time
The form asks how you spent time outside class this week. More hours do not automatically mean better learning. Very long study time can crowd sleep. Do not tell every student to study more.
Current scoring uses two kinds of items when they are on the form:
- Question 1 (ST1): weekly study hours. Choices: Less than 5 hours, 5–10 hours, 11–15 hours, 16–20 hours, More than 20 hours.
- Question 2 (ST2): how often you review. Choices: Never, Sometimes, Often, Very Often.
Some forms still also ask hours per day on assignments, reviewing lessons, and focused study sessions.

2. Q1. On average, how many hours per day did you study this week? / weekly study hours
Weekly choices: Less than 5 hours, 5–10 hours, 11–15 hours, 16–20 hours, More than 20 hours.
How this may feel: Long study hours can make it feel like you are working hard but still falling behind.
Advice for next week: Do not add more hours. Sit for 40 to 50 minutes with one written goal, then take a real break. If those hours stole sleep, shorten the latest session rather than adding another. If hours are not high, do not assume they must study more.

3. Q2. How many hours per day did you spend on assignments and projects this week?
On some forms this second item is how often you review (Never, Sometimes, Often, Very Often).
How this may feel: Assignments and projects can take the whole day, with several files open and none finished. Reviewing very often can blur together.
Advice for next week: Finish a small piece of the assignment due soonest instead of opening every project at once. Split outline, draft, check, submit. If the question is about reviewing often, test yourself with a few questions instead of rereading.

4. Q3. How many hours per day did you spend reviewing lessons this week?
How this may feel: Hours of reviewing can blur together when you keep rereading instead of testing yourself.
Advice for next week: Test yourself with a few questions, cards, or say one idea out loud. Stop rereading for hours. Short reviews across days beat one long cram.

5. Q4. How many hours per day did you spend on focused study sessions this week?
How this may feel: Sitting a long time is not the same as real focus, and rest can get squeezed out.
Advice for next week: One chapter or worksheet, phone away, 40 to 50 minutes, then stand up. Write one sentence before you sit: what will be done when this block ends.

6. ADVICE WHEN STUDY TIME IS HIGH
If sleep is also a concern, protect rest first and shorten a late session.
Mention the words from the form: hours you studied, assignments and projects, reviewing lessons, focused study sessions, or how often you review.
Never tell them to study all night or sleep less so they can study more.

7. ADVICE WHEN STUDY TIME IS MODERATE
One efficiency tip and one real break. Quality beats adding more hours.

8. ADVICE WHEN STUDY TIME LOOKS OKAY
One kind sentence only. Do not assume they are not trying.

9. STUDENT-FACING GUIDANCE
Write like a caring adviser. Use "you." Use simple, basic words. Short sentences. Do not diagnose.
Match advice to the Study Time questions the student answered. Do not start every tip with "If you."

HOW THIS MAY FEEL
- Q1: Long weekly study hours can make it feel like you are working hard but still falling behind.
- Q2: Hours on assignments and projects, or reviewing very often, can leave several files open and none finished.
- Q3: Hours reviewing lessons can blur together when you keep rereading.
- Q4: Long focused study sessions are not the same as real focus, and rest can get squeezed out.

THINGS YOU CAN TRY NEXT WEEK
- Q1: Do not add more hours. Sit for 40 to 50 minutes with one written goal, then take a real break. Make tonight's last study shorter if it is stealing sleep.
- Q2: Finish a small piece of the assignment due soonest instead of opening every project at once.
- Q3: Test yourself with a few questions or say one idea out loud. Stop rereading for hours.
- Q4: Write one sentence before you sit: what will be done when this block ends. Phone away, then stand up after 40 to 50 minutes.
- Progress can be one finished piece, not staying up all night.
